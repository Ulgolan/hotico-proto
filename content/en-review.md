#### Phase — Procedure

- **Title:** Procedure
- **Body:** _(null)_

- **Item 1:** 1. Personalized analysis and preparation. We sit down, discuss your expectations and desires, and I analyze your skin and the area to be treated, taking into account your medical history, skin type, and undertone. Then we draw the ideal shape together, analyze it visually, and adjust it until we both feel it is perfect for you. We choose the right shade, a pigment that will blend naturally with your skin.

- **Item 2:** 2. Design and pigment implantation. Once we have established the shape, I apply the topical anesthetic and then begin the first lines or guide dots, drawn with very low intensity. I wipe away the temporary outline and apply another topical anesthetic, preparing the skin for the actual pigmentation stage. Then I work in controlled layers, adjusting the pressure, depth, and density of the pigment according to the type and condition of the skin so that each area has the necessary intensity.

- **Item 3:** 3. Completion and post-procedure care. After completing the procedure, we analyze the result and explain step by step how to care for the pigmented area during the healing period. Depending on your skin type and the procedure, you will receive personalized recommendations for maintenance so that the result remains natural, stable, and beautiful over time.


#### Phase — Touch-up

- **Title:** Touch-up
- **Body:** _(null)_

- **Item 1:** 1. Post-healing analysis. After 4-6 weeks, when the skin is completely healed, we meet again to perform the touch-up. We analyze together how the pigment has settled and discuss any adjustments: shape, shade, or final intensity. At this stage, you play an active role because, after observing how the color has gradually changed during healing, you can now clearly express your preferences regarding the desired final intensity.

- **Item 2:** 2. Retouching. This is the consolidation stage, in which we intervene again on the area, applying pigment where necessary for uniformity and stability. The procedure is the same as the first session, but without redrawing the shape, we are already working with a beautiful, agreed base, which we are perfecting. The goal is to fix the color and provide a uniform, natural, and durable result.

- **Item 3:** 3. Color refreshment. After a few years, depending on skin type and procedure, I recommend a maintenance session. At this stage, the color is gently refreshed to maintain a fresh and harmonious appearance. We do not redraw or reconstruct, we only revitalize the pigment so that the result remains natural and elegant over time.

#### Q4. "What you need to do before areola dermopigmentation"

- **Question:** "What you need to do before areola dermopigmentation"

>
> Areola reconstruction or repigmentation is a delicate procedure with a huge emotional impact. Whether we are talking about a post-mastectomy context, scars after reduction or augmentation, burns, or the correction of congenital asymmetry, proper preparation of the skin and body is the first step toward a beautiful and stable result over time.
>
> First of all, the area must be completely healed. If you have undergone surgery, the general recommendation is to wait at least 6–12 months until the scars become flat, light in color, and no longer sensitive to touch. In the case of burns, the waiting time may be even longer, until the doctor confirms that the skin has completely healed.
>
> Skin hydration is essential. In the weeks leading up to the procedure, apply a nourishing cream or oil to the breast area daily (avoiding direct application to very recent scars) to restore elasticity and prevent dryness. Supple, healthy skin receives pigment more evenly and fixes it better.
>
> Avoid sun exposure or tanning beds on the breast area for at least 4 weeks before the procedure. Tanning, whether natural or artificial, can change the skin tone and influence the choice of pigment color.
>
> If you are undergoing medical treatment (e.g., hormone therapy after breast cancer or anticoagulant treatment), please let me know in advance and discuss it with your doctor. In certain situations, it may be necessary to adjust the timing of the procedure for your safety.
>
> On the day of your appointment, avoid coffee, alcohol, and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (aspirin, ibuprofen), as they can increase skin sensitivity and the risk of bleeding. Wear loose-fitting, soft clothing that does not rub against the breast area and cause irritation.
>
> As a professional, I will carefully analyze the area on the day of the procedure: skin texture, tone, scars, and breast characteristics. We will discuss the ideal shade and technique to achieve a natural three-dimensional effect that will restore not only your aesthetics but also your confidence. If I notice that your skin is not ready—for example, it is too sensitive, too dry, or there is redness/inflammation—we will reschedule. Not for the sake of unnecessary delay, but because I want the final result to be flawless and long-lasting.
>
> Preparing for areola micropigmentation is not just a technical step, but also an emotional one. It is the moment when you prepare your body and mind for a change that often marks the end of a difficult chapter and the beginning of a new one. And I am here to make this moment not only beautiful, but also completely safe.


#### Q5.  What happens immediately after and how do I maintain the procedure?

- **Question:**  What happens immediately after and how do I maintain the procedure?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/Cj3-EfPse04
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134711774/de054ab2bf?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

>
> After areola micropigmentation, a delicate healing period begins, during which your body needs time and care for the final result to be exactly as we want it.
> The area may be slightly sensitive, especially if it has undergone previous surgery, such as mastectomy or breast reconstruction. It is absolutely normal to experience slight redness or a feeling of warmth in the first few days.
>
> It is very important to keep the area clean and dry. I will provide you with a special ointment, which you will apply in a thin layer, according to my instructions. This helps protect the skin and fix the pigment. Avoid any cream, lotion, or oil that I have not recommended to prevent irritation or premature fading of the color.
>
> Do not rub, scratch, or remove any scabs that may appear. They are part of the natural healing process and, if forced, can also remove the pigment, leaving small gaps in the color.
>
> In the first few weeks, avoid intense sports, swimming, saunas, and hot baths, as excess heat and humidity can affect pigment fixation. If it is summer or if the area could be exposed to the sun, make sure you wear clothing that completely covers your chest. After healing, always protect the area with high SPF sunscreen.
>
> It is important to know that everyone's skin works differently, especially when it comes to an area that has undergone medical procedures. Therefore, monitoring progress and retouching when necessary are part of the complete process.
>
> I will be with you after the procedure to ensure that the result remains uniform, beautiful, and stable over time. Proper care in the first few weeks makes all the difference for a natural and harmonious effect that you can enjoy for years to come.


#### Q6. Why are two procedures necessary?

- **Question:** Why are two procedures necessary?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/3hGfw3MSc4A
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134714625/d065d5d624?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

> This is a question I get asked very often, and it's natural for you to have it in mind too. The short answer is: because every skin is unique and reacts differently to pigment.
>
> After the first dermopigmentation session, the treated area, whether it's the eyebrows, lips, or areolas, looks more intense. The pigment has just been deposited, and the skin is slightly inflamed. Over the next few weeks, during the natural healing process, the color begins to fade and settle until it reaches its final shade.
>
> During this period, the skin naturally eliminates a percentage of the pigment. This is the body's normal reaction to any element introduced from outside. This is where retouching comes in: it is the second session in which we fill in the areas where the pigment has settled less, even out the color, adjust the symmetry if necessary, and strengthen the contours.
>
> Retouching is essential for a uniform, long-lasting, and natural-looking result. Once the skin has "gotten used" to the pigment during the first procedure, it will accept it much better during the second, which makes the final result stable and beautiful over time.
>
> In short: the first procedure prepares the ground, and the touch-up completes the job. Without touch-ups, the result is only half done. With touch-ups, you get the eyebrows, lips, or areolas you want, for longer and without surprises.

#### Q2. Microblading vs Hair stroke dermopigmentation?

- **Question:** Microblading vs Hair stroke dermopigmentation?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/Yo3jhzJLu-g
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134712970/afbee760a0?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

> Microblading vs. hair-by-hair. This is a question I get asked very often, and it's important to understand the differences because they affect not only the appearance of your eyebrows but also the long-term health of your skin.
>
> Microblading is a manual technique in which the specialist uses a thin blade to make small cuts in the skin, into which they deposit pigment. Although the result may seem natural at first, the process is invasive to the skin. Complete healing can take up to 6 months, and during this time your body struggles to eliminate the pigment, considering it a foreign body. That is why the results of microblading fade quickly, sometimes in as little as 6–12 months. When retouching, the same cuts are made in the same area, increasing the risk of scarring, thinning skin, and an uneven appearance over time.
>
> Hair-by-hair dermopigmentation with a dermograph works completely differently. Instead of incisions, the device uses extremely fine micro-needles (only 0.18 mm), which penetrate superficially through controlled vibration and create tiny channels in the form of dots, into which the cosmetic pigment is deposited. This means that the skin is not cut, but delicately worked, and healing is much faster, usually between 28 and 40 days.
>
> In addition, the dermograph allows me to precisely adjust the depth, speed, and direction of the needle according to your skin type and the desired result. Thus, the threads created perfectly mimic the thickness, direction, and density of your natural threads, and the final result is much finer, safer, and more durable.
>
> The pigment's lifespan is also superior: with hair-by-hair dermopigmentation, your eyebrows retain their beauty and naturalness for up to 2–3 years, without the risk of scarring or unsightly color changes.
>
> In short: microblading cuts the skin and offers shorter-lasting and riskier results, while hair-by-hair dermopigmentation with a dermograph respects the skin, heals faster, lasts longer, and offers a flawless natural look. It is the choice I confidently recommend for beautiful, healthy, and harmonious eyebrows in the long term.

#### Q1. What is lips dermopigmentation?

- **Question:** What is lips dermopigmentation?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/5mVL5l7AcaQ
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134711822/0140c6e130?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

> A smile is one of the most beautiful expressions we wear, and the color and contour of the lips can give it more harmony and radiance. Lip dermopigmentation is a technique by which I delicately restore or intensify the natural shade of the lips, correct small asymmetries, and create a fine contour, so that they always look full of life.
>
> It is an ideal choice if your lips are very pale, have lost pigment over time, or if you want to even out the color or give them a fresher and more defined look without using lipstick every day.
>
> The procedure is performed using a professional dermograph and special pigments, chosen in shades that match your skin tone and complement your features. The final result can be very discreet, for a natural "my lips but better" effect, or more intense, depending on your wishes.
>
> Lip dermopigmentation does not mean a strong, rigid color, but a transparent, warm shade that blends perfectly with the texture of your lips. The pigment gradually fades over time, allowing you to adjust or refresh the color whenever you feel the need.
>
> The end result? Lips with a more defined contour, uniform color, and healthy appearance that will make your smile stand out in the most beautiful way, every day, effortlessly.


#### Q2. What kind of pigments do I use?

- **Question:** What kind of pigments do I use?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/wchI4eoAIe0
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134711847/ca1efbf32c?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

>
> When it comes to lips, pigment is everything. This is an area with a higher pH, rich vascularization, and a natural shade that varies greatly from person to person, so color selection must be extremely careful and personalized.
>
> I use professional, biocompatible, and hypoallergenic cosmetic pigments, specially formulated to last on the lips without changing tone over time. These pigments are designed to preserve the beauty of the color and fade gradually and elegantly, so you can make adjustments when you want a refresh.
>
> When selecting the shade, I look at the tone and undertone of your skin: warm, cool, or neutral, the natural color of your lips, their natural pigmentation (more uniform or with paler areas), and the desired final effect: maybe you want a natural look, like your lips, but fresher, or maybe you want a more saturated color, inspired by your favorite lipstick.
>
> An important detail: in the case of lips with very cold or dark natural pigment (as sometimes happens due to genetic causes or after certain treatments), tone neutralization sessions may be necessary before applying the desired color. This ensures a uniform and beautiful result.
>
> In the end, the pigment we choose together is the perfect combination of safety, durability, and harmony with your natural beauty.


#### Q3. Is the procedure painful?

- **Question:** Is the procedure painful?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/kqk8FgxrJ5M
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134714613/53378a3329?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

>
> I know this is one of the first questions that crosses your mind before scheduling a procedure. And that's normal, no one wants to go through unnecessary discomfort.
>
> The truth is that everyone feels differently. Some clients tell me that it feels like a slight sensation, like a fine scratch or gentle tingling. Others may feel it a little more intensely, but very rarely does anyone describe the procedure as painful.
>
> Compared to classic tattoos, cosmetic or paramedical dermopigmentation only works on the superficial layers of the skin, which automatically means less discomfort.
>
> In addition, I use topical anesthetics: creams, gels, or liquids applied directly to the skin, which serve to make the whole process as comfortable as possible. These may contain:
>
> Lidocaine, for rapid numbing,
> Tetracaine, for a deeper effect,
> Benzocaine, sometimes combined with the others,
> and epinephrine, which reduces bleeding and inflammation and prolongs the anesthetic effect.
> Throughout the procedure, I am attentive to how you feel. If necessary, I can apply additional anesthetic so that we can work at your pace and with the utmost care for your body.
> In short: it is not completely sensation-free, but it is a procedure designed to be gentle, safe, and bearable, so that your experience is as relaxed and stress-free as possible.


#### Q4. Can I do this while breastfeeding?

- **Question:** Can I do this while breastfeeding?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/0NxmDb_DaDo
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134714593/f808dbf7db?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

>
> This is one of the questions I get asked most often, and I want to give you a sincere and complete answer. In theory, cosmetic dermopigmentation only acts on the superficial layers of the skin, using biocompatible pigments and local anesthetics applied to the surface. I work with sterile, single-use consumables in an environment that complies with all hygiene and safety standards.
>
> However, according to best practice recommendations, all aesthetic procedures, including dermopigmentation, are contraindicated during pregnancy and breastfeeding. The reason? The precautionary principle. Even if the risk of adverse reactions is low, the body undergoes significant hormonal and physiological changes, and any additional stress or local reaction can influence your general condition and, indirectly, breastfeeding.
>
> My recommendation, as a professional, is to postpone the procedure until after breastfeeding or at least until the last few months, when breastfeeding is no longer exclusive. It is the safest choice for both you and your baby.
>
> Your safety and health always come first. Dermopigmentation is about beauty, but above all about care, responsibility, and respect for your body.


#### Q5. What you need to do before lips dermopigmentation?

- **Question:** What you need to do before lips dermopigmentation?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/8iDUKg_dei0
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134711831/6c523f503f?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

>
> Before I pigment your lips, I want to make sure we start with a perfect base. Why? Because beautifully prepared lips retain pigment better, heal more evenly, and help us achieve exactly the shade you want.
>
> The first and most important step: hydration. If your lips are dry, with scabs or flaky skin, the pigment will not penetrate the skin evenly and, inevitably, the result will be uneven. At least 7-10 days before your appointment, apply an emollient and repairing balm (with ingredients such as shea butter, coconut oil, beeswax, or panthenol) several times a day. At night, you can apply a thicker layer for a "moisturizing mask" effect.
>
> If you are prone to herpes (on the lips or around the mouth), talk to your doctor about preventive antiviral treatment (e.g., acyclovir), starting a few days before. This is extremely important because the procedure can trigger an episode, even if you haven't had one in a long time.
>
> In the week before the procedure, avoid harsh peels or abrasive lip scrubs. If you want to exfoliate gently, use a mild exfoliant with fine sugar or honey, but only in the first few days of the week, not right before your appointment.
>
> On the day of your appointment: avoid coffee, green tea, energy drinks, and alcohol, as they can all sensitize and dry out your lips. Also, avoid blood-thinning medications (aspirin, anti-inflammatories, omega-3 supplements, ginkgo biloba) without letting me know in advance.
>
> Very important: if your lips are very dry and crusty on the day of the procedure, we will not be able to work on them. The procedure will be postponed to protect the final result. Our goal is for you to have evenly pigmented lips that heal beautifully and without complications.
>
> And there's something else I love to tell my clients: come with clean, natural lips, without makeup and with an open mind. I bring all the know-how, you bring the preparation, and together we make your lips exactly the shade and shape that will enhance your smile in the most beautiful way.


#### Q6.  What happens immediately after and how do I maintain the procedure?

- **Question:**  What happens immediately after and how do I maintain the procedure?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/mvw6P-rRa0I
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134711813/3c65d2ade5?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

> After getting lip micropigmentation, the healing period begins, which is just as important as the procedure itself in order to achieve a beautiful and even result.
> In the first few hours, your lips may appear more intense in color than you imagined, and this is perfectly normal. The pigment is still on the surface, and over the next few days, the color will gradually fade to its final shade. During this time, you may also feel a slight sensation of tightness or dryness.
>
> Very important: do not rub, scratch, or touch your lips with dirty hands. If fine scabs appear, let them fall off on their own. Forcibly removing them can also remove the pigment, leaving lighter areas.
>
> Apply a balm or cream recommended by me several times a day to keep your lips moisturized and protected. Avoid perfumed or mentholated products, as they can irritate the area.
>
> In the first few days, avoid very hot, very spicy, or acidic foods, as they can cause stinging and affect the healing process. Try to drink liquids through a straw to minimize contact with your lips.
>
> Very important: do not expose your lips to the sun, tanning beds, or strong sources of heat. If you must go outside, use a balm with a high SPF after your lips are completely healed.
>
> Lip makeup is completely prohibited until the healing process is complete. After that, you can use it if you wish, but you will find that most of the time it will no longer be necessary—the pigment will give you a beautiful and natural effect every day.
>
> I will stay in touch with you after the procedure to monitor how your lips are healing and to determine together the optimal time for a touch-up. With care, patience, and following the care steps, the color will set beautifully and you will enjoy it for a long time.


#### Q7. Why are two procedures necessary?

- **Question:** Why are two procedures necessary?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/3hGfw3MSc4A
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134714625/d065d5d624?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

>
> This is a question I get asked very often, and it's natural for you to have it in mind too. The short answer is: because every skin is unique and reacts differently to pigment.
>
> After the first dermopigmentation session, the treated area, whether it's the eyebrows, lips, or areolas, looks more intense. The pigment is freshly deposited, and the skin is slightly inflamed. Over the next few weeks, during the natural healing process, the color begins to fade and settle until it reaches its final shade.
>
> During this period, the skin naturally eliminates a percentage of the pigment. This is the body's normal reaction to any element introduced from the outside. This is where the touch-up comes in: it is the second session in which we fill in the areas where the pigment has settled less, even out the color, adjust the symmetry if necessary, and strengthen the contours.
>
> Touch-ups are essential for a uniform, long-lasting, and natural-looking result. Once the skin has "gotten used" to the pigment during the first procedure, it will accept it much better during the second, which makes the final result stable and beautiful over time.
>
> In short: the first procedure prepares the ground, and the touch-up finishes the job. Without touch-ups, the result is only half complete. With touch-ups, you get the eyebrows, lips, or areolas you want, for longer and without surprises.


#### Q8. How long does the pigment last?

- **Question:** How long does the pigment last?
- **YouTube:** https://youtu.be/SVCiTSEpoMc
- **Vimeo:** https://vimeo.com/1134711857/0612346b14?fl=ls&fe=ec
- **Answer:**

>
> Lips have a special charm when they retain their shape and color, and dermopigmentation helps you enjoy this look effortlessly, year after year. Typically, beautiful results last between 3 and 5 years, sometimes even longer.
>
> An important reason for this durability is that the chosen pigment is very close to the natural shade of the lips, and the fading over time is discreet. In addition, the rich vascularization of the lips helps the pigment to fix evenly and retain its pleasant appearance longer than in other areas of the face.
>
> Of course, longevity also depends on you: constant hydration, sun protection with SPF specifically for lips, and avoiding habits such as smoking can significantly prolong the beauty of the result.
>
> And when you feel that the shade is losing its intensity, a light touch-up restores freshness and brightness without having to repeat the entire procedure. This way, your lips remain contoured and naturally colored long after the first session.
